Newton's law of universal gravitation states that every particle in the universe attracts every other particle with a force proportional to the product of their masses and inversely proportional to the square of the distance between them. This law, along with his three laws of motion, forms the foundation of classical mechanics, which accurately describes the motion of macroscopic objects at speeds much slower than light. Classical mechanics remained the dominant framework for physics until the early 20th century, when relativity and quantum mechanics revealed its limitations.
